Minnesota teen driver license rules

By Which Driving Test. Last checked 16 Nov 2026.

Not a government site. Not affiliated with any DMV. This is general information, not legal advice. Rules and fees change. Check your state licensing agency before you apply. Read the disclaimer.

Teen licensing in Minnesota is the graduated path published by Minnesota Department of Public Safety. Adult applicants can have different rules.

An instruction permit is available at 15, 16, or 17 after driver education steps, vision and knowledge tests, and an approved application. A provisional license is available at 16 after the permit has been held and a supervised driving log is submitted. The first six months of that provisional license limit late-night driving and young passengers. Teen rules (official site)

Quick answer

  • Learner permit: The department may issue an instruction permit to an applicant who is 15, 16, or 17 years of age and who:.
  • First license: has reached the age of 16 years;.
  • Practice hours: at least 50 hours behind the wheel supervised by a parent or guardian, including 15 hours at night.
  • Night rule: For the first six months, a provisional license holder may drive between midnight and 5:00 a.m. only in the situations the statute lists, including trips between home and work.
  • Passengers: For the first six months, a provisional license holder may not carry more than one passenger under 20 who is not immediate family. The limit does not apply when a parent or guardian is in the vehicle.
